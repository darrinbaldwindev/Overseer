## Executive assessment

The lane supports a **plausible operating model**, not an investment conclusion. A ghost kitchen can reduce front-of-house and storefront requirements, but the model remains sensitive to local food permits, facility capacity, labor, packaging, order density, and third-party delivery economics. A franchise adds a separate diligence and compliance layer: the U.S. Federal Trade Commission (FTC) says a franchisor must provide a Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) containing 23 specified information items, and the prospective franchisee must receive it at least 14 days before signing or paying. Public web claims about low startup costs, margins, territories, brand partnerships, Walmart placement, or franchise availability are **not substitutes for an FDD, executed agreements, local approvals, or independently verified operating results**.

## Scenario calculations (illustrative estimates, not forecasts)

The only numerical inputs below are public claims from the Breadless page, not verified operating data. They are included to make sensitivities explicit.

1. **Startup range:** midpoint of the claimed $30,000–$80,000 range is `(30,000 + 80,000) / 2 = $55,000`. This midpoint is a simple arithmetic estimate, **not** a likely cost. A separate directory listing states $100,000 minimum cash, which is not directly comparable: “minimum cash” may include liquidity requirements rather than total project cost, and the listing itself requests confirmation.
2. **Commission sensitivity:** for every $100 of gross marketplace sales, a 15–30% commission range implies $15–$30 before food, labor, packaging, rent, royalties, advertising, refunds, taxes, and other costs. The spread is $15 per $100 of marketplace sales. This demonstrates sensitivity only; it does not establish any platform’s current terms.
3. **Channel-mix illustration:** if a hypothetical $10,000 monthly gross sales mix were 70% marketplace and 30% direct/catering, and if the unverified commission range applied only to marketplace sales, commission expense would be `$10,000 × 70% × 15%–30% = $1,050–$2,100` per month. This is a scenario, not an estimate of actual sales or fees; no marketplace terms were verified.
4. **Claimed margin translation:** a claimed 15–20% net margin on hypothetical $10,000 monthly sales would equal $1,500–$2,000 monthly net income. Because the source does not provide a complete, audited unit P&L in the page reviewed, this must not be used for valuation, financing, or earnings representation.

## Findings by required status

### VERIFIED FACT

- The FTC Franchise Rule requires franchisors to provide prospective franchisees a disclosure document with 23 specified information items.
- FTC guidance says the FDD must be delivered at least 14 days before the prospective franchisee is asked to sign a contract or pay money to the franchisor or an affiliate.
- FTC guidance identifies key diligence areas including startup/ongoing costs, purchasing restrictions, territory and e-commerce restrictions, advertising/training, Item 19 financial performance claims, Item 20 outlet turnover and franchisee contacts, Item 21 audited financial statements, and Item 22 proposed agreements.
- FDA guidance identifies clean/separate/cook/chill as core safe-food practices and provides food-temperature guidance; it is general public guidance, not a site permit.
- The Breadless article was visibly marked “Last updated: August 23, 2026”; the FTC FDD article is dated May 24, 2023; FDA page is dated March 5, 2024. These dates were observed on 2026-09-18.

### REASONABLE INFERENCE / ESTIMATE

- Removing a dining room and reducing equipment can **potentially** lower capital and occupancy requirements relative to a full-service storefront, but savings depend on the leased facility, local code, utilities, equipment, rent, and required upgrades.
- A hybrid of direct pickup/catering and marketplace delivery could reduce exposure to per-order marketplace fees compared with marketplace-only sales; the size of any benefit is unknown.
- The $55,000 midpoint and the commission examples above are arithmetic scenarios useful for diligence questions, not forecasts or representations.
- A franchise decision should be gated on an actual, current FDD and proposed agreements rather than public landing-page claims.

### UNKNOWN / BLOCKED

- No current FDD, franchise agreement, Item 7 investment table, Item 19 performance representation, Item 20 outlet/turnover data, Item 21 audited financial statements, royalty schedule, advertising fund terms, supplier rebates, or territory grant was obtained.
- No site, city/state, health department, zoning, fire, grease, hood/ventilation, wastewater, accessibility, insurance, or permit review was performed; local requirements can materially change cost and timing.
- No authenticated delivery-platform contract, commissions, payment processing, advertising terms, merchant onboarding status, customer volume, ranking, or marketplace permission was established.
- No evidence establishes Prime status, wholesale pricing, brand licensing/permissions, national-brand participation, Walmart placement, current franchise availability, earnings, legal certainty, or security posture. Public directory statements and promotional claims were not independently corroborated.
- No independent franchisee interviews or financial-record verification was conducted, and no outreach was made by design.

## 3–5 next safe actions

1. **Request and archive the current FDD without paying or signing.** Confirm delivery date and preserve Items 1–23 plus all exhibits; enforce the FTC 14-day review window before any commitment.
2. **Build a site-specific permit and capex checklist.** Ask the relevant local authority (through an authorized owner/operator process) about food establishment permitting, plan review, zoning, fire, grease, ventilation, wastewater, accessibility, and inspection requirements; obtain written quotes rather than relying on generic ranges.
3. **Reconcile economics to documents and records.** Test Item 7 against equipment/lease quotes and Item 19 against store-level P&Ls; separately model food, labor, packaging, rent, royalties, advertising, delivery fees, refunds, taxes, and working capital under low/base/high order volumes.
4. **Verify rights and channel terms before relying on them.** Require written evidence of brand licenses, supplier obligations, territory/e-commerce rules, and marketplace merchant terms; do not assume permission or preferred status from a public listing.
5. **Use Item 20 contacts for independent diligence, without coercion or paid reviews.** Compare recent and former franchisees’ opening delays, actual investment, support, closures, break-even experience, and channel mix; document discrepancies and escalate material uncertainty to legal/accounting advisers.
