## Executive finding

**VERIFIED FACT:** Australia has a substantial and growing online-shopping audience. Australia Post reports that Australians spent **AUD 82.6 billion online in 2025**, up 14% year on year, and that **9.8 million households** shopped online. It also reports an average basket size of **AUD 96**, 16 brands purchased per household, and four additional annual online purchases compared with the prior year. These figures support demand for useful comparison, review, and buying-guide content, but they do not establish that an affiliate website will rank, receive traffic, be accepted into a program, or earn revenue.

**VERIFIED FACT:** The ACCC says ordinary advertising rules apply to social-media promotions, including posts involving incentives to influencers. Claims about price, product characteristics, benefits, performance, delivery, and shipping must be truthful and accurate. Ad Standards states that brand arrangements must be disclosed, including arrangements involving free products or services; clear labels such as `#Ad`, `#sponsored`, or a clear caption are examples. These rules are relevant to affiliate links and sponsored content, but this report is not legal advice and does not determine compliance for a particular site.

**VERIFIED FACT:** Amazon Australia publicly describes its Associates program as allowing creators, publishers, and bloggers to earn from qualifying purchases and programs, and advertises “up to 12%” in associate commissions. “Up to” is not a universal rate, and the public landing page does not establish the rate for a particular product, account, traffic source, or date. The eBay disclosure FAQ could not be read in this environment because its page presented a browser-check interstitial; therefore eBay terms, rates, cookie rules, and eligibility remain **UNKNOWN/BLOCKED** here.

**REASONABLE INFERENCE/ESTIMATE:** A low-cost, defensible test would focus on one narrow Australian audience and high-intent content such as product comparisons, buying guides, and price/feature explainers. The principal risks are not market size but acquisition cost and uncertainty: search and social distribution are competitive, commissions vary by partner and category, returns or cancellations can reverse commissions, and program terms can change. No earnings, availability, Prime status, marketplace permission, wholesale pricing, or legal certainty is inferred.

## Scenario table and calculations

The following is an **illustrative sensitivity model**, not a forecast and not evidence of any partner’s actual rate. It isolates the variables that must be measured before scale.

| Monthly qualified sessions | Affiliate click-through rate | Merchant conversion rate | Average order value | Illustrative commission rate | Illustrative orders | Illustrative gross commission |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10,000 | 3% | 2% | AUD 96 | 3% | 6 | AUD 17.28 |
| 10,000 | 5% | 3% | AUD 96 | 5% | 15 | AUD 72.00 |
| 25,000 | 5% | 3% | AUD 96 | 5% | 37.5 | AUD 180.00 |

Calculation: **gross commission = sessions × click-through rate × merchant conversion rate × average order value × commission rate**. The second row, for example, is 10,000 × 0.05 × 0.03 × AUD 96 × 0.05 = AUD 72. Actual outcomes can be lower because of attribution windows, excluded products, returns, cancellations, invalid traffic, geographic restrictions, tax, payment fees, and program-specific caps. No scenario should be treated as expected earnings.

A useful break-even check is: **required monthly orders = fixed monthly operating cost ÷ net commission per order**. If a site had AUD 150 in fixed monthly costs and an illustrative AUD 4.80 net commission per order (AUD 96 × 5%, before other deductions), it would require 31.25, or at least 32, orders per month. This is only arithmetic using assumptions; it is not a claim about achievable traffic or commissions.

## What is not established

The research does **not** establish authenticated wholesale prices, affiliate acceptance, current commission schedules for a specific account or product, Prime status, retailer inventory, product availability, marketplace or API permission, earnings, ranking potential, conversion rates, cookie duration, attribution treatment, payout thresholds, or legal certainty. It also does not establish that any brand permits paid search, email, coupon, social, price comparison, AI-generated content, or use of its trademarks. The eBay page was technically blocked by a browser-check interstitial. Detailed Amazon operating terms were not used as a substitute for account-specific terms.

## 3–5 next safe actions

1. **Create a read-only partner-term register.** For each candidate program, record the official application URL, current terms URL, allowed traffic sources, disclosure language, attribution rules, exclusions, reversals, payout threshold, and observed date. Do not apply or authenticate until owner approval exists.
2. **Validate one narrow content hypothesis without publishing.** Draft a small set of comparison and buying-guide outlines using only independently verifiable product facts. Add a disclosure pattern and a claim-evidence checklist. Do not copy marketplace content or imply live price, stock, Prime, warranty, or delivery status without a current source.
3. **Build a measurement plan before launch.** Define qualified session, outbound click, merchant conversion, approved commission, reversal, and cost metrics. Use conservative ranges rather than a single earnings estimate, and keep partner analytics read-only.
4. **Obtain an Australian compliance review.** Ask qualified counsel or a registered tax professional to review Australian Consumer Law claims, AANA/Ad Standards disclosure practice, Privacy Act and tracking implications, income-tax/GST treatment, and any foreign-source withholding questions.
5. **Run a bounded go/no-go review.** Require evidence of approved partner access, permitted traffic sources, compliant disclosures, data-handling controls, and a documented break-even case before any publication or production change. Active repository project lineages remain read-only.
