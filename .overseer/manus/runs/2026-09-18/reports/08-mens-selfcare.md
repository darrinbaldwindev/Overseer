## Executive assessment

**VERIFIED FACT:** Brisbane is a large, diverse service market. The 2021 Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) Census recorded 1,242,825 residents in the Brisbane local government area (LGA), with 49.2% male, a median age of 36, and 67.9% labour-force participation among people aged 15 and over. The same Census recorded median weekly household income of $2,068 and 25.6% of occupied private dwellings as apartments. These facts support testing a convenient, repeat-visit men’s grooming/self-care offer, but they do not establish demand, willingness to pay, or a viable site. [1]

**REASONABLE INFERENCE/ESTIMATE:** A concept combining barbering with selected add-on self-care services could plausibly fit Brisbane’s working-age, apartment-dense catchments if it is positioned around convenience, repeat booking, and credible service quality. The inference is directional only. It is not a forecast of sales or profitability.

**VERIFIED FACT:** An existing Australian competitor, Mancave Barbershop, publicly markets a franchise model and states that it has “15 profitable stores across Australia,” more than 10 years of experience, location and lease support, marketing support, and supplier relationships. These are statements made by the franchisor on its own website, not independently audited facts. The page does not publish authenticated franchise fees, full investment, royalties, store-level earnings, Brisbane territory availability, or Prime/marketplace status. [5]

**VERIFIED FACT:** Queensland Government guidance states that franchising can involve restrictions on suppliers, products, territories, fit-out, and operating decisions, and warns that franchises are not guaranteed to succeed. It says prospective franchisees should receive five key documents at least 14 days before signing or making a non-refundable payment. [3] The ACCC regulates the Franchising Code but does not provide legal advice or resolve individual disputes. [4]

## Illustrative capacity calculation (not a forecast)

A simple operating model can make the next diligence step concrete without claiming market performance. If one chair completes **8 paid visits per day**, operates **6 days per week**, and has **48 trading weeks per year**, annual service capacity is:

`8 × 6 × 48 = 2,304 visits per chair per year`.

With **3 chairs**, illustrative capacity is **6,912 visits per year**. This is capacity, not demand or revenue. It excludes no-shows, staff absence, ramp-up, seasonality, service mix, opening hours, and chair utilization. Revenue would require verified service prices and a tested utilization rate; neither is asserted here.

## Next safe actions

1. **Build a Brisbane catchment screen** using Council/ABS SA2 and SA3 data. Compare population, age, income, apartments, daytime-worker proxies, transport, and visible competitor counts for 3–5 candidate areas. Keep every metric sourced and dated.
2. **Collect public competitor evidence** from official menus and location pages only. Record service categories, displayed prices, booking availability, opening hours, memberships, and review counts without creating accounts or contacting businesses.
3. **Request and review franchise documents only through an authorised owner-approved process.** Before any payment or commitment, obtain the information statement, franchise agreement, disclosure document, disclosure fact sheet, and code copy, then have an independent franchise lawyer and accountant review them. The 14-day document timing described by Queensland guidance is a minimum process point, not a guarantee of suitability. [3]
4. **Create a conservative unit-economics model** with explicit inputs for chairs, hours, staff cost, rent, fit-out, consumables, royalties, marketing levy, payment fees, insurance, tax, no-shows, and ramp-up. Run downside, base, and upside scenarios without using franchisor earnings claims.
5. **Validate the service/legal boundary before testing sales.** Confirm council, Queensland health, practitioner qualification, product, privacy, employment, insurance, and workplace-safety requirements for each proposed service. Do not launch, accept deposits, or collect personal data until approvals and ownership are clear.
