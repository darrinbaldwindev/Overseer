# Sunshine Coast Car Rental Economics

**Batch:** Overseer 2026-09-18  
**Lane:** Sunshine Coast car rental economics  
**Observed:** 18 September 2026 (AEST)  
**Scope:** Public, current evidence for demand, market structure, and selected operating-cost inputs. This is not a forecast, investment recommendation, legal opinion, or evidence of access to authenticated wholesale rates.

## Executive assessment

**VERIFIED FACT:** Sunshine Coast Airport reported strong recent passenger growth: 458,000 passengers in July–September 2025, 17% above the same quarter in 2024, and 160,000-plus passengers in July 2025 versus 135,000 in July 2024. The airport also stated that Sydney and Melbourne represented 88% of airline seats and that it expected more than 1.8 million passengers in FY2026. These facts support a growing addressable arrival stream, but they do not establish rental-car conversion, utilisation, achievable rate, or profit.

**VERIFIED FACT:** The airport presents a dedicated rental-car centre about a two-minute walk from the terminal and lists self-service and off-airport providers including RaD Car Hire, East Coast Car Rentals, Bespoke Noosa Car Rentals, Apex Car Rentals, KINTO, and Aussie Bargain Car Rentals. This confirms visible competition and multiple fulfilment models; it does not establish market shares, airport concession terms, or marketplace permission.

**VERIFIED FACT:** Queensland’s published 12-month 4-cylinder light-vehicle registration guide, effective 1 July 2026, totals $864.50–$877.50 including registration, the traffic improvement fee, and a Class 1 CTP premium range. That is approximately $2.37–$2.40 per calendar day before insurance beyond CTP, depreciation, finance, cleaning, maintenance, tyres, parking/airport fees, labour, telematics, damage, downtime, and taxes.

**REASONABLE INFERENCE/ESTIMATE:** At the ACCC’s observed Sunshine Coast unleaded petrol price of 220.8 cents/litre on 15 April 2026, a vehicle consuming 7.0 L/100 km would incur about $15.46 of fuel per 100 km. This is a sensitivity input, not a fleet-wide cost: actual fuel use, fuel policy, kilometres driven, and the applicable fuel price vary.

The public evidence indicates a plausible demand tailwind but is insufficient to underwrite unit economics. The largest unobserved variables are realised daily rate by vehicle class and season, booked days per available day, vehicle acquisition and residual value, insurance and damage cost, airport access/parking/concession charges, cleaning and turnaround labour, and channel commissions.

## Scenario calculations (illustrative, not a forecast)

The following calculations isolate a few observable inputs. They deliberately exclude revenue and most costs because those inputs are not publicly verified here.

### Annual registration allocation

For a 4-cylinder vehicle:

- Lower annual statutory guide: **$864.50**.
- Upper annual statutory guide: **$877.50**.
- Daily equivalent: **$864.50 / 365 = $2.37** to **$877.50 / 365 = $2.40** per calendar day.
- Monthly equivalent using 12 equal months: approximately **$72.04–$73.13**.

This is an ownership-cost floor for the listed components only. It is not a complete daily fleet cost and should not be treated as a break-even rate.

### Fuel sensitivity

Using the ACCC observation of **$2.208/L** and an illustrative consumption of **7.0 L/100 km**:

`7.0 L / 100 km × $2.208/L = $15.456 per 100 km`, or approximately **$15.46 per 100 km**.

For a 200-km rental, the same assumptions imply approximately **$30.91** of fuel consumed. This is an operator cost only if the operator supplies fuel without full recovery; customer fuel policy and refuelling fees can change the cash economics.

A simple sensitivity at the same fuel price is:

| Consumption assumption | Fuel cost per 100 km |
|---:|---:|
| 5.5 L/100 km | $12.14 |
| 7.0 L/100 km | $15.46 |
| 9.0 L/100 km | $19.87 |
| 12.0 L/100 km | $26.50 |

These are **REASONABLE INFERENCE/ESTIMATE** calculations, not observed fleet averages.

### Demand-to-fleet framing

The airport reported 458,000 passengers for one quarter. If a future analyst wants a bounded demand scenario, the correct next step is to apply explicit, independently sourced assumptions for rental-party share, average passengers per party, airport pickup share, and rental length. For example, a hypothetical 5% rental-party share would be 22,900 parties, but that percentage is invented for sensitivity only and must not be presented as observed demand. No occupancy, utilisation, average daily rate, or revenue estimate is asserted in this report.

## What remains unknown or blocked

The following cannot be established from the accessible public evidence and must not be inferred:

1. **Authenticated wholesale pricing, marketplace permission, or Prime status.** No logged-in supplier, marketplace, or platform account was accessed. Public comparison snippets are not a substitute for contractual or authenticated pricing.
2. **Real-time availability and realised rates.** The comparison page was blocked by bot verification. Search snippets do not reveal search dates, inventory, vehicle class, taxes, excess, deposit, or cancellation terms.
3. **Utilisation and seasonality.** Airport passenger growth does not prove rental conversion, booked days, fleet utilisation, or shoulder-season performance.
4. **Commercial fleet cost treatment.** The Queensland fee page is a private-use guide. Commercial registration, insurance, CTP class, airport access, and concession arrangements require direct confirmation from official or contractual sources.
5. **Total cost of ownership.** Vehicle purchase price, depreciation, financing, residual values, maintenance, tyres, cleaning, labour, damage, theft, telematics, roadside assistance, and downtime are not evidenced.
6. **Revenue quality and earnings.** No operator earnings, owner income, booking records, take rate, platform commission, or net margin is known.
7. **Legal certainty.** This lane does not determine licensing, consumer-law, insurance, airport, tax, privacy, or roadworthiness compliance. Those are separate professional reviews.
8. **Security and operational controls.** No claims are made about keyless access, identity verification, deposits, fraud controls, or vehicle-tracking security.
