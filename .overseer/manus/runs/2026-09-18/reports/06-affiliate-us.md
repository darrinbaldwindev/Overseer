## Executive conclusion

A U.S. affiliate website is technically and commercially feasible only as a **compliance- and quality-dependent content business**. The clearest verified constraints are: affiliate relationships must be disclosed clearly and close to the recommendation; a program operator can withhold commissions for agreement violations; Google can suppress low-value, scaled, scraped, or manipulative content; and tax and privacy obligations depend on the operator’s facts. These facts do not establish approval by any affiliate network, traffic, conversion, earnings, product availability, Prime status, wholesale pricing, marketplace permission, or legal sufficiency for a particular site.

The safest near-term posture is **research and a reversible pilot specification**, not launch. Ownership and lineage in the repository were treated as read-only; no project state was inspected or changed.

## Classification of findings

### VERIFIED FACT

1. FTC guidance requires clear, conspicuous affiliate disclosure close to the recommendation and says “affiliate link” alone may be inadequate. [1]
2. Amazon’s public U.S. agreement requires compliance and properly tagged Special Links, and reserves the ability to stop commission payments for violations. [2]
3. Google’s public spam policy identifies scaled low-value content and manipulative links as search-policy risks, while recognizing appropriately qualified paid links. [3]
4. IRS resources expressly cover self-employed and small-business recordkeeping, deductions, returns, and payment topics relevant to a possible affiliate operator. [4]
5. The California AG describes CCPA rights and threshold-based applicability for qualifying for-profit businesses. [5]

### REASONABLE INFERENCE / ESTIMATE

1. A small pilot with a narrow topic and original evaluation is lower operational risk than a large automated catalog because it is easier to review disclosures, sourcing, link attributes, and content quality before scaling. This is a risk-management inference from the cited policies, not a guarantee of rankings or compliance.
2. The scenario table demonstrates that traffic and conversion assumptions dominate revenue sensitivity. It does not forecast demand.
3. Keeping an evidence log for prices, availability, claims, disclosures, and link destinations should reduce stale or unsupported statements. This is a recommended control, not a verified requirement of every program.

## Next safe actions

1. **Owner review:** confirm the intended domain, audience, entity, and named owner before any implementation; preserve repository lineages as read-only.
2. **Compliance specification:** draft a reversible content template containing a near-link affiliate disclosure, claims-evidence fields, privacy notice requirements, and a link-attribute rule; obtain qualified legal/tax review for the actual facts.
3. **Authorized program verification:** through the owner’s existing authorized account, verify current program acceptance, permitted traffic sources, link formats, commission rules, tax forms, and product-data permissions. Do not infer these from public marketing pages.
4. **Small offline pilot:** prepare a limited set of candidate topics and an evidence ledger without publishing or contacting merchants. Review every product claim, date, price, availability statement, and outbound destination before any owner-approved publication.
5. **Measurement gate:** define success and stop thresholds for qualified traffic, disclosure QA, link integrity, reversals, and cost before launch; do not treat the illustrative scenarios as targets or forecasts.
