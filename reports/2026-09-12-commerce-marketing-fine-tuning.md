# GlobalShopCo + Shopify→eBay Marketing Fine-Tuning — 2026-09-12

**Owner:** Marketing Overseer  
**Status:** CURRENT DIRECTION / SKU-EVIDENCE-GATED

## GlobalShopCo positioning

**Primary promise:** Useful products, carefully selected.

The store should not feel like an undifferentiated dropshipping catalogue. Marketing should make selection quality, practical utility and straightforward shopping the point of difference.

### Message hierarchy
1. Useful for a real everyday problem.
2. Carefully selected rather than endlessly listed.
3. Clear price, delivery and returns information.
4. Free delivery where the current offer supports it economically.
5. Shopify-backed checkout and customer journey.

### Recommended hero direction
**Useful products. Carefully selected.**
Practical finds for everyday life, without the endless scrolling.

CTA: **Browse useful products**

### Product-content formula
Problem → practical use → why this product made the shortlist → current delivery/returns facts → CTA.

Avoid leading with supplier brand, sourcing process or wholesale economics; those support trust internally but are not the customer job.

## Catalogue discipline

Marketing should promote fewer products more repeatedly once evidence clears, rather than constantly adding new low-proof items. A strong product should earn:
- hero/static creative;
- problem-solution short video;
- comparison/carousel;
- category guide inclusion;
- email/social follow-up;
- eBay variant where appropriate.

A candidate without freight evidence does not become a marketing priority merely because it looks attractive.

## Shopify→eBay positioning

**Primary promise:** Practical products selected for eBay shoppers.

This feed should be more transactional than GlobalShopCo main, but still useful rather than deal-spam.

### eBay post hierarchy
1. What problem does it solve?
2. Why is it useful?
3. What are the current listing facts?
4. Why consider this one over obvious alternatives?
5. View the verified listing.

### Avoid
- generic “must-have” language;
- artificial scarcity;
- unverified “best price” claims;
- claiming local/Australian stock without current evidence;
- posting a SKU before freight/returns/listing facts are verified.

## Category priority refinement

For marketing, prioritise categories that are:
- visually demonstrable;
- low explanation burden;
- low regulatory/compliance risk;
- parcel-friendly;
- useful in everyday life;
- able to preserve contribution after delivery costs.

This generally favours home organisation, kitchen/pantry organisation, pet accessories, cleaning/household utility and selected mobile/computer utility before safety-sensitive or bulky categories unless stronger evidence exists.

## Content ratio

For every 10 commerce posts:
- 4 problem-solution/product utility posts
- 2 comparisons or selection guides
- 1 trust/delivery/returns post
- 1 category education post
- 1 behind-the-selection post
- 1 direct conversion post

This keeps the brand from feeling like a stream of product ads.

## Free-delivery wording

Treat free delivery as a verified offer attribute, not the core brand promise. Customer-facing copy can use **Free delivery** when current checkout/listing conditions support it. Internal SKU selection must continue to model freight economics; marketing must not use free delivery to rescue an otherwise weak product proposition.

## First conversion test

Test:
A: product-first landing pages with direct CTA.
B: problem/guide-first landing pages that introduce the product after explaining the use case.

Track product-page CTR, add-to-cart, conversion, bounce and return/refund signals. Promote whichever path produces stronger qualified conversion, not merely more clicks.
