## Asset 1 — Small pantry reset: organise by access, not by container count

### Search intent
`small pantry organisation`, `how to organise a small pantry`, `pantry storage ideas Australia`

### Core angle
A useful pantry is not the one with the most organisers. Start with how often items are used, their shape, spill risk and whether you need to see stock at a glance.

### Structure
1. Put daily-use items between waist and eye level.
2. Group by task: breakfast, lunch prep, baking, snacks, back-up stock.
3. Use shallow storage where forgotten items are the real problem.
4. Measure shelf depth, door clearance and handle clearance before buying anything.
5. Leave 10–20% breathing room so the system survives grocery day.
6. Label only where labels reduce decisions; avoid turning every shelf into maintenance work.

### Safe CTA
`Browse our Home Organisation guides as we qualify practical storage options for Australian homes.`

## Asset 2 — Fridge bins: when clear storage helps and when it wastes space

### Search intent
`fridge organiser bins`, `are fridge bins worth it`, `fridge organisation ideas`

### Core angle
Clear bins help when they create zones and stop small items disappearing; they hurt when a bulky bin consumes more shelf volume than the food it contains.

### Buyer/reader checklist
- Measure usable shelf width, depth and height.
- Check whether lids are actually useful for the intended food.
- Prefer stackability only if stacked access remains practical.
- Consider airflow and manufacturer guidance for produce.
- Avoid sets with many tiny pieces you cannot assign to a real use.
- Compare total usable internal volume, not just “number of pieces”.

### Safe CTA
`Use the checklist first; product options should fit the space rather than force the space to fit the product.`

## Asset 3 — Under-sink storage without creating a plumbing headache

### Search intent
`under sink organiser`, `under kitchen sink storage`, `small kitchen storage ideas`

### Core angle
The best under-sink layout works around pipes, valves, leaks and cleaning-product safety. A rigid “perfect rectangle” storage system often fails because the cabinet is not actually rectangular once plumbing is included.

### Structure
1. Measure around P-trap, water lines and shutoff valves.
2. Keep valves reachable without unloading the cabinet.
3. Put leak-sensitive items above the cabinet floor where practical.
4. Separate chemicals from food-contact items and children’s access.
5. Use removable rather than permanent storage where plumbing service access matters.
6. Choose width/height around the actual pipe map, not the cabinet's external size.

### Safe CTA
`Measure the obstruction map before comparing storage options.`

## Asset 4 — Drawer organisers: the 60-second test before you buy

### Search intent
`drawer organiser`, `kitchen drawer organiser`, `how to organise drawers`

### 60-second test
1. Empty the drawer.
2. Put back only items used at least monthly.
3. Group by action, not appearance.
4. Measure internal width/depth/height including tapered sides.
5. Count the widest and longest items.
6. Decide whether adjustable compartments solve a real size mismatch.

### Editorial point
A drawer organiser is useful when it reduces search time and stops objects migrating. It is not useful if fixed compartments force awkward storage or waste half the drawer.

### Safe CTA
`Measure first. Organise second. Buy last.`
