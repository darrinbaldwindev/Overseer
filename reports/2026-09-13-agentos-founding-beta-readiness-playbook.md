# AgentOS Founding Beta — Readiness & Launch Playbook

**Status:** HOLD UNTIL INVOKED / PREPARATION COMPLETE IN PRINCIPLE  
**Owner:** Marketing Overseer / ChatGPT Overseer  
**Product authority:** AgentOS Product / Engineering  
**Security / assurance authority:** Jack / Henry / Green / PRS as applicable  
**Created:** 2026-09-13  
**Purpose:** Durable launch playbook to be called on when AgentOS is ready for controlled external beta testing.

---

## 1. Purpose

This document exists so the AgentOS Founding Beta can be launched deliberately when the product is ready, without rebuilding the recruitment, onboarding, security, legal, communications, testing, and evidence plan from scratch.

The working beta identity is:

> **AgentOS Founding Beta**  
> **Give AgentOS a real job.**

The beta is not primarily a marketing campaign. It is a governed evidence programme intended to answer whether ordinary users, professionals, creators, and technical users can safely understand, trust, and repeatedly use AgentOS for real work.

The beta must not begin merely because recruitment channels are available. Product readiness controls launch.

---

## 2. Launch Trigger

Call on this playbook only when the AgentOS Overseer determines that a beta candidate build exists and the minimum entry gate below can be assessed.

### Minimum entry gate

Before external recruitment begins, require evidence for all applicable items:

- installer / setup path exists for the intended beta platform;
- beta build/version is pinned and reproducible;
- stop / pause / revoke controls are present and understandable;
- permission boundaries are defined;
- no known unresolved S0 safety / trust issue;
- first-job flow works sufficiently for internal testers;
- failure states do not report false success;
- data-handling behavior is documented;
- known limitations are written in plain language;
- support / incident route exists;
- beta privacy notice is prepared;
- beta terms / NDA decision is approved;
- incentives are approved and operationally deliverable;
- missions are mapped to features actually shipped in the beta build;
- an internal dry run has been completed with at least 2–3 people before external Wave 0.

If these conditions are not met, beta status remains **HOLD**.

## 3. Beta Objective

The first external beta should determine:

1. whether users understand what AgentOS is;
2. whether users can install and start it without excessive assistance;
3. whether they can give AgentOS a real job;
4. whether AgentOS understands that job;
5. whether permissions make sense;
6. whether users know how to stop activity;
7. whether the result is useful;
8. whether evidence / assurance is understandable;
9. where trust breaks;
10. whether users return for a second real job without prompting;
11. whether different user segments receive different kinds of value;
12. what should be fixed before broader release;
13. whether the current Free / $39 Co-worker / $99 Operator ladder matches perceived value.

The beta must not be judged by vanity sign-up numbers.

## 4. Cohort Model

Target final initial programme size: approximately **30–35 testers**, staged rather than released all at once.

### Wave 0 — Controlled local pilot

**Target:** 5–8 testers  
**Geography:** Sunshine Coast / Brisbane preferred  
**Purpose:** catch installer, permissions, trust, Windows, onboarding, and first-job failures before scale.

Suggested mix:
- 2 everyday / non-technical;
- 2 professional / small business;
- 1 creator / power user;
- 1–3 technical / developer.

### Wave 1 — Mixed Australian cohort

**Target:** approximately 10–12 additional testers  
**Purpose:** validate repeat use, real workflows, broader hardware / skill diversity, and onboarding comprehension.

### Wave 2 — International specialist expansion

**Target:** approximately 12–15 additional testers  
**Geography:** Australia + US + UK + selected global English-speaking communities  
**Purpose:** test technical edge cases, multi-AI workflows, local AI, broader use cases, and geography-independent onboarding.

### Wave 3 — Public waitlist / public beta preparation

Only after stop/go gates pass.

Possible channels later:
- Product Hunt;
- BetaList;
- Reddit beta communities;
- AI / local-model communities;
- maker / developer communities;
- creator / professional communities;
- broader organic social;
- paid acquisition only after evidence supports it.

## 5. Target Tester Mix

Approximate mix for a 30-person cohort:

- **Everyday / non-technical:** 10
- **Professional / small business:** 8
- **Creator / power user:** 7

Reserve at least 8 seats for users with low technical confidence.

> **What is one real task you would like AgentOS to help you get done?**

A concrete real job is more valuable than enthusiasm alone.

## 9. NDA / Confidentiality Decision

An NDA is **not automatically required for every beta**.

Before launch, Product / Owner should choose one of three models:

### Option A — No NDA

Use when:
- most tested behavior can safely be public;
- secrecy would reduce participation;
- public discussion is acceptable;
- product risk comes mainly from reliability, not unreleased trade secrets.

Required instead:
- beta terms;
- privacy notice;
- responsible disclosure / no credential-sharing requirement;
- restriction on publishing security-sensitive findings before coordinated review.

### Option B — Light confidentiality agreement

Recommended default for early controlled beta if unreleased screenshots, technical behavior, pricing experiments, roadmaps or security-sensitive details matter.

Should cover:
- non-public product details;
- unreleased builds;
- screenshots / recordings;
- security findings;
- internal roadmaps / pricing tests;
- credentials / access links;
- duration of confidentiality;
- exclusions for information already public or independently known;
- permitted disclosure to professional advisers where lawful.

### Option C — Formal NDA

Use only where justified by highly sensitive IP, partner obligations, enterprise integrations, confidential commercial terms or significant unreleased security architecture.

**Important:** final NDA / beta terms should be reviewed by qualified legal counsel for the jurisdictions involved. This document is an operational plan, not legal advice.

### NDA rule

Do not use an NDA to suppress legitimate security reporting, consumer rights, protected disclosures, or truthful reporting of harm where law protects it.

## 10. IP / Feedback Ownership

Beta terms should make clear, in plain language:

- tester owns their pre-existing content;
- AgentOS / owner retains product IP;
- tester grants permission for submitted feedback, bug reports, suggestions and anonymised findings to be used to improve AgentOS;
- no obligation exists to implement suggestions;
- public testimonials require separate explicit permission;
- feedback reward is for participation, not assignment of unrelated personal IP;
- source code / build files may not be redistributed if the beta build is private.

Avoid unnecessarily broad IP-assignment clauses that discourage serious testers.

## 11. Security & Privacy Concerns

Security handling is a first-class part of the beta.

### Before launch

Prepare:

- beta security overview;
- what AgentOS can access;
- what AgentOS cannot access;
- permission model;
- how credentials are handled;
- storage locations;
- local vs cloud behavior where applicable;
- telemetry / diagnostics policy;
- logs collected;
- retention period;
- who can access beta reports;
- deletion / revocation route;
- uninstall / cleanup procedure;
- emergency disable / kill path where applicable.

### Tester instruction

Testers should be told not to use:

- production customer secrets;
- banking credentials;
- passwords;
- private keys;
- API keys unless the approved setup explicitly requires them through secure entry;
- medical / highly sensitive personal information;
- confidential employer / client data unless explicitly approved for that testing path;
- live production infrastructure they are not authorised to test.

### Credential handling

Never ask testers to paste secrets into chat, forms, screenshots or bug reports.

Where BYO API keys are supported:

- use secure application entry;
- mask secrets in UI and logs;
- document revocation steps;
- never transmit keys to a feedback form;
- testers should preferably use restricted / low-limit keys for beta testing.

### Least privilege

Permissions should be scoped to the minimum required for the current task.

A beta is not justification for broad permanent access.

## 12. Security Research / Vulnerability Reporting

Technical testers may discover genuine vulnerabilities.

Provide a separate private security-report path.

Required fields:

- affected build/version;
- environment;
- description;
- impact;
- reproduction steps;
- logs / screenshots where safe;
- whether exploitation required privileged access;
- whether the issue is still active.

### Security severity

Use the existing beta severity framework:

- **S0 — Safety / trust critical**
- **S1 — Blocking**
- **S2 — Major**
- **S3 — Minor**
- **S4 — Cosmetic / suggestion**

S0 security / trust findings stop cohort expansion.

No public security disclosure should be solicited before a coordinated review path exists.

---

## 13. Data Separation

Beta data should be separated from production wherever practical.

Prefer:

- beta-specific environment;
- beta-specific accounts / entitlements;
- restricted scopes;
- non-production fixtures;
- test-only external integrations where available;
- explicit labels in logs;
- separate support / incident records.

Do not silently turn beta users into production users.

---

## 14. Known-Limitations Document

Every build distributed externally must include a dated list of known limitations.

Examples may include, only when true:

- unsupported Windows versions;
- local-only behavior;
- unavailable providers;
- scheduler disabled;
- remote execution unavailable;
- limitations on Night Shift;
- recovery limitations;
- MCP/tool gaps;
- known model/provider compatibility problems;
- logging / UI limitations.

Marketing must not hide known limitations.

## 17. First Job Protocol

Show:

> **What is something you genuinely need to get done?**

Record:

- task as entered;
- expected outcome;
- whether AgentOS understood it;
- rewrites / clarification required;
- permissions requested;
- execution outcome;
- evidence / assurance shown;
- whether the result was useful;
- user confidence after the task.

If a tester cannot think of a real job, provide segment-safe starter examples but record this as a positioning signal.

1. Ask
2. Create
3. Organise
4. Multi-step
5. Repeat
6. Recover
7. Trust

## 19. Feedback System

For each mission, collect at minimum:

1. What were you trying to accomplish?
2. What did you expect?
3. What happened?
4. Was the result useful? Yes / No / Partial.

Optional follow-ups:

- where confused;
- what felt uncomfortable;
- permission comprehension;
- whether stop controls were understood;
- remaining manual work;
- whether they would repeat the workflow;
- what would stop them;
- how they normally do the task.

Do not force long surveys after every successful mission.

## 20. Trust Log

Track separately from general bugs:

- permission felt too broad;
- unexpected action;
- more autonomy than expected;
- unclear result / provenance;
- weak evidence;
- assurance contradicted perceived reality;
- stop / pause unclear;
- uncomfortable access request;
- false success;
- action continued after expected stop.

Every trust event receives rapid review.

## 21. Incident Response

### Immediate STOP conditions

Pause further invitations / distribution if any of these occur:

- S0 safety / trust issue;
- credential exposure;
- unauthorised data access;
- unexpected destructive action;
- inability to stop activity reliably;
- repeated false-success reporting;
- repeated broad-permission confusion with material risk;
- suspected cross-user data leakage;
- serious installer / uninstall damage;
- security vulnerability with credible material impact.

### Incident workflow

1. preserve evidence;
2. stop affected distribution / capability if required;
3. notify responsible product/security authority;
4. classify severity;
5. identify affected build / testers;
6. provide safe mitigation instructions;
7. implement fix;
8. independently verify exact fix;
9. decide whether affected testers need notification;
10. record durable incident receipt;
11. only resume after authorised GO decision.

Do not minimise trust incidents because the software is "only beta."

Recommended completion criteria:

- install completed;
- at least 2 real jobs attempted;
- Day 7 checkpoint completed;

Reward is for genuine participation, not praise.

State explicitly:

> **Negative or critical feedback does not affect your reward. We need honest reporting more than positive reporting.**

Do not promise lifetime access.

## 24. Pricing Research During Beta

Do not lead testers with internal pricing assumptions.

Near the end, ask in this order:

1. Would you continue using AgentOS?
2. Would you pay for it?
3. What price would feel fair?
4. Which capabilities felt most valuable?
5. Which capabilities should be Free / Co-worker / Operator?
6. Which tier best matches what you actually used?

Then reveal the proposed ladder:

- Free
- $39/year Co-worker
- $99/year Operator

Then ask again for reaction.

This preserves an unanchored value signal before price anchoring.

Do not use unsupported "pays for itself" or guaranteed-savings language.

## 26. Stop / Go Gate Between Waves

### STOP / HOLD if

- any unresolved S0;
- repeated false success;
- serious data-handling concern;
- stop controls are unreliable;
- first-job path fails systematically;
- installer failures materially prevent participation;
- permissions are repeatedly misunderstood in risky ways;
- the current build is not representative enough to learn from.

### GO only when

- serious findings are understood;
- fixes have evidence;
- affected fixes have been re-tested;
- first-job completion is reliable enough for the next cohort;
- onboarding is understandable;
- current cohort findings are being incorporated rather than merely collected;
- Product / Overseer explicitly authorises expansion.

Numeric thresholds should be set against actual internal baseline performance immediately before launch rather than permanently hard-coded here.

## 34. Final Operating Principle

The Founding Beta should optimise for **truth before growth**.

A small cohort that exposes installer failure, permission confusion, false success, weak trust, or genuine repeated value is more useful than hundreds of early sign-ups.

The best beta tester is not the person most excited by AI.

It is the person with a **real job**, a real expectation, and enough honesty to tell us exactly where AgentOS succeeds or fails.
