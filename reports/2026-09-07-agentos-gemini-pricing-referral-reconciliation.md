# AgentOS Marketing Overseer — Gemini Pricing + Referral Reconciliation

**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Owner:** Marketing Overseer
**Status:** AMBER — HYPOTHESIS / VALIDATION REQUIRED

## Purpose

Record the Marketing Overseer reconciliation of Gemini's independent commercial challenge covering $29 vs $39 pricing, $99 differentiation, member referrals, onboarding, Night Shift, creator attribution and referral economics.

This record distinguishes accepted recommendations from rejected or unsupported claims.

## Working pricing position

### Preferred current commercial hypothesis

**Free → $39/year → $99/year → subscriptions / BYO AI / specialist capability**

- **$39/year** becomes the preferred working challenger and likely replacement candidate for the earlier $29/year concept if validation is favourable.
- **$99/year** remains the intended Operator destination.
- $39 is **not final approved pricing**. It remains a commercial hypothesis pending real conversion, retention, cost and upgrade evidence.

### Why $39 is currently stronger than $29

RECOMMENDATION / INFERENCE:

- $39 gives more revenue headroom if a user never upgrades.
- $39 reduces the nominal gap to $99 from $70 to $60.
- The $39 → $99 step is approximately 2.54x versus approximately 3.41x for $29 → $99.
- Pure topline break-even: if conversion is the only variable, $39 produces at least as much revenue per visitor as $29 whenever the $39 conversion rate is at least about 74.4% of the $29 conversion rate (29/39).

This is arithmetic, not product-market evidence. Profitability still depends on actual cost-to-serve and user behaviour.

## $39 tier — working product role

The $39 tier should not become “$99, but you have to click Run yourself.”

Working role: **Co-worker / materially capable interactive AgentOS with a limited taste of autonomy.**

Recommended differentiators include:

- materially more capability than Free;
- larger AI/resource envelope;
- more memory/context;
- more multi-step work;
- more integrations;
- limited schedules and/or limited Night Shift;
- limited queue/concurrency;
- standard recovery/checkpointing;
- more user involvement than $99.

## $99 tier — working differentiation

The $99 Operator tier should be broader across multiple dimensions, not differentiated only by a single Night Shift switch.

Recommended dimensions:

- substantially greater bounded autonomy;
- multiple Autonomy Hours / Night Shift routines;
- more recurring background work;
- multiple schedules;
- larger job queues;
- greater concurrency;
- larger persistent Context Spaces / cross-job memory where implemented;
- broader integrations;
- larger AI/resource envelope;
- advanced checkpoint/resume/recovery;
- materially less required user involvement.

Universal trust controls must not be paywalled away: stop/pause, basic permissions, revocation, privacy controls and core assurance remain available across tiers.

## Night Shift position

Gemini recommended making unattended Night Shift effectively exclusive to $99. Marketing Overseer does **not** adopt that recommendation.

Current recommendation:

- $39 should receive a **limited taste of Night Shift / scheduled autonomy** so users can experience the value that may later motivate a $99 upgrade.
- $99 should substantially expand autonomy scope, recurrence, concurrency, schedule count, context, recovery and capacity.

The middle tier should demonstrate the magic rather than hide it completely.

## Member referral programme — accepted direction

The member referral programme remains separate from the creator/influencer affiliate programme.

### Best referral trigger

Primary trigger:

**First successful verified Morning Brief / Night Shift outcome**, where the user has experienced a clear autonomous-value moment.

Fallback trigger:

**Several successful multi-step jobs across multiple active days** for users who have not yet experienced Night Shift.

Referral prompts should be value-triggered, not shown at signup.

## Referral rewards — working direction

Marketing Overseer prefers **product-native rewards first** rather than immediate cash-equivalent discounts or large account credits.

Candidate rewards:

- extra Night Shift runs;
- extra AI allowance;
- temporary additional workflow capacity;
- temporary specialist capability;
- limited Operator-style autonomy credits.

Rationale:

- potentially high perceived value;
- potentially lower direct economic cost than cash credits;
- lower risk of immediate margin destruction;
- can demonstrate paid-tier benefits.

Cash/account-credit rewards remain a later test candidate once real gross margin, compute cost, fraud and upgrade data exist.

## Referral qualification — accepted direction

A two-stage qualification model is retained as a useful working design.

### Stage 1 — engagement qualification

Possible qualifying events:

- 3 meaningful verified jobs; or
- 1 successful qualifying Night Shift.

Result: small product-native reward can be issued.

### Stage 2 — commercial qualification

Referred user upgrades to a paid tier.

Result: a larger reward may be issued to the referrer, subject to validated economics and anti-abuse controls.

Raw signup rewards are rejected.

## Referral message

Current strongest desired organic word-of-mouth sentence remains:

> **“Instead of figuring out which AI to use, I just give AgentOS the job.”**

This is a target user expression, not an evidence-backed superiority claim.

Preferred official top-of-funnel framing remains simpler:

> **Put AI to work.**

Supporting explanation should avoid unsupported “best,” “easiest,” “maximum quality,” or guaranteed savings claims.

## Referral landing page — working direction

The referred-user page should avoid overwhelming the visitor with models, tier architecture, technical governance or all four characters at once.

Working structure:

- Headline: **Put AI to work.**
- Supporting explanation: AgentOS helps plan, carry out and check work using the AI available to the user, subject to actual implemented capabilities.
- CTA: start free / start first job.
- Referral benefit shown clearly if one exists.
- Referrer identity may be shown in a simple privacy-respecting form where appropriate.

## Creator affiliate interaction

Gemini introduced a 30-day creator attribution window in part of its response. This is rejected because it conflicts with the existing working AgentOS affiliate hypothesis.

Current working hypothesis remains:

- **90-day creator attribution**;
- creator/affiliate receives cash/revenue-share attribution under the agreed affiliate rules;
- ordinary member referrer receives product-native reward or account credit rather than a second full cash commission;
- avoid two full cash payouts for the same transaction unless explicitly authorised as a deliberate commercial strategy;
- $39 → $99 upgrade attribution should preserve the original creator attribution within the applicable 90-day rules, subject to final affiliate policy.

## Onboarding relationship

Retain the latest Marketing Overseer direction:

- all four roles introduced within the first week where user behaviour permits;
- introductions should be task-triggered rather than rigidly date-gated;
- 30 days remains the nurture window;
- after Day 30, value-based upgrade reminders may appear approximately every 7 days when there is a fresh, relevant reason;
- avoid repetitive generic nagging.

Canonical role definitions remain unchanged:

- **Willow** — understands/plans;
- **Isla** — executes;
- **Jack** — permissions/risk/authority;
- **Henry** — assurance/checking.

## Explicitly rejected / unsupported Gemini claims

The following numerical claims were not accepted as evidence because no source or validated AgentOS data supported them:

- 15–20% higher conversion at $29;
- 5–10% lower impulse volume at $39;
- $12–$15 annual orchestration cost;
- $14–$17 or $24–$27 net margin/user;
- $0.15 Night Shift compute cost;
- $0.30–$0.50 Night Shift API cost;
- specific LTV figures;
- specific CAC reductions;
- specific referral K-factor revenue outcomes;
- claims that referral automatically improves 90-day retention.

These may be used only as hypothetical scenario inputs if clearly labelled, never as current AgentOS facts.

## Next commercial validation

Recommended next experiment:

1. Test **$29 vs $39** as alternative middle-tier offers.
2. Test referral prompting only after a **verified aha moment**.
3. Prefer product-native referral rewards first.
4. Measure actual conversion, paid-tier mix, $39 → $99 progression, retention, reward cost, abuse and referral activation.
5. Do not declare $39 final until real evidence exists.

A fixed 14-day duration is not itself the success criterion. The experiment should run until the cohorts provide enough evidence for a credible comparison.

## Final Marketing Overseer status

**AMBER — coherent commercial direction, but pricing, referral economics and upgrade behaviour remain unvalidated.**

Current preferred working ladder:

**Free → $39/year → $99/year → subscriptions / BYO AI**

Current referral principle:

**Ask after demonstrated value; reward meaningful activation, not raw signup.**
